# The Dunk Program

**Final Evolution LLC · Coach: Elijah Bonds**, professional dunker.
NASM Certified Personal Trainer (CPT), NASM Corrective Exercise Specialist (CES), NASM Performance Enhancement Specialist (PES), FMS Level 1, NESTA Biomechanics Specialist.

Three tracks, one finish line. Your Mirror screen picks where you start:
- **Track 1 · Correctives:** knees, ankles and depth.
- **Track 2 · Static & Dynamic Posture:** stack, turn and carry.
- **Track 3 · Dunking & Plyometrics:** spring, approach and the rim.

Tracks 1 and 2 hand you to Track 3 when you pass their exit checks. Everyone ends in Track 3.

> Every number here is a **coach target, not a norm**. It's where I aim my athletes. Your own trend matters more than any target.

---

## 1. Who it's for

- Athletes 18+ who want to dunk, or dunk higher, more often, and with better landings.
- Ages 13–17 only when a parent or guardian buys it and gives them a code. Teens follow the teen limits in §4. A teen's progress stays on the teen's phone, and teens see only their jump number from the screen, not bands.
- Basketball, volleyball and track athletes who want a better approach jump.
- No accounts for under 13.

## 2. The outcome

- A Mirror screen at day 1, 14, 28, 42 and 56, so you can see your flags change.
- Your standing jump, approach jump and arm swing score tracked every two weeks with the jump tracker.
- A clean landing pattern, a stacked trunk, and a repeatable approach.
- A dunk session at day 56, filmed and counted.

**What I don't promise:** inches, or that you'll dunk by a date. Results vary. We re-screen you so you see what changes.

## 3. Prerequisites

- Complete the Mirror screen before day 1 (phone on a stand, full body in frame, plain background).
- Able to do 10 bodyweight squats and 20 easy two-foot pogos with no pain.
- Access to: a resistance band (light and medium), a foam roller, a low step or box (15–30 cm), one dumbbell or kettlebell, a wall, and a hoop for Track 3 dunk days. A trap bar or barbell with safety pins is used in strength weeks if you have one; the dumbbell versions are listed.
- 3 sessions a week of about 35–60 minutes, plus two 5-minute Movement Snacks.

## 4. Safety notes and when to refer out

- **Pain means stop.** Sharp, pinching, or getting-worse pain: stop that drill and see a medical pro before you train that area again. Next-day soreness is normal. Pain during the rep isn't.
- This is coaching, not medical care. **It doesn't diagnose or treat anything**, and it can't promise you won't get hurt. The screen is where we start, not a diagnosis.
- **Refer out before starting or continuing** if you have: pain that wakes you at night; numbness, tingling or weakness down a leg; a joint that gives way; swelling after training; a recent sprain, fracture or surgery; heel or knee-cap pain that's lasted more than two weeks; chest pain, fainting or unusual shortness of breath; or leaking, pain or heaviness in the pelvic floor (see a pelvic health physio; the pelvic floor cue here is a coaching cue only).
- **Breathing:** no long breath holds. Brace, then breathe out on the effort or on landing. "Brace, don't freeze."
- **Teen limits (13–17):** Under 18: most sessions use 30-50 jump contacts. FEL never goes above 55 in one session. 2–3 days a week, about 110 contacts a week and under 900 for the whole 8 weeks (Elijah's rule). No rung 5 or 5C, no loaded jumps, and dunk sessions stay at 10 attempts or fewer. Overcoming isometrics are allowed.
- **Box drop jumps (rung 5C)** are adults only and unlocked only by me in a live or video review.
- **Slept under 6 hours?** Train lighter and skip max-effort jumps that day.

## 6. How I fix things: Down, Up, Check

**Calm it, lengthen it, wake it up, put it to work.** That's the corrective order I use as a NASM Corrective Exercise Specialist.
- **Down:** roll or pin-and-stretch what's overworking, on a long exhale. Self-myofascial release (SMR).
- **Up:** a band pulls you into your mistake, and your body pulls you out. That's reactive neuromuscular training (RNT).
- **Check:** redo your screen move and compare before and after. Better? Keep it. Same? Change the spot or band angle.

*Open the door, then walk through it.* Rolling opens a short window of new range; the band drill walks you through it so your brain learns it's yours. "Mixing the down with the up" is a phrase from **Judith Aston's** postural work (*Aston Postural Assessment*). The pairing and words here are mine.

### 6.2 Breath and stack (intra-abdominal pressure, cavity coupling)

**Ribs down, pelvis under. Stack the can.** When your rib cage sits over your pelvis, the diaphragm sits over the pelvic floor and each breath builds pressure all the way around your trunk. Pressure stiffens your trunk so force travels through it instead of leaking. **Brace, don't freeze.**

The ladder, 3–5 breaths per position:
1. Lying 90/90 (feet on a wall, hips and knees at 90°).
2. Hands and knees.
3. Half-kneeling.
4. Split stance.

Each breath: in through the nose about 3–4 s into your belt (front, sides, low back), no chest rise. Out about 6–8 s until the low ribs drop. A gentle pelvic floor lift on the exhale.

**RNT split-stance row and press:** 2 × 8 each side, rest 30 s. The band pulls you into rotation; ribs stay over the pelvis with real upper-back extension. Breathe out on the pull and the press.
**Exit test:** 3 × 8 each side, steady breath, no pelvic shift, two sessions in a row.

### 6.3 The FEL plyo ladder (oscillatory plyometrics)

Small, quick bounces teach your legs to be springy. You move up by **quality, not the calendar**. Both feet landing together count as one contact.

| Rung | Name | Drills | Sets × contacts | Rest between sets | Per session | Move up when (two sessions in a row) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Land | Snap-down to stick; step off a low step and stick | 3–4 × 5 | 60 s | 15–25 | Knees over toes, quiet landing, 3 s stick |
| 1 | Bounce | Two-foot pogos in place, 15–20° knee bend | 3–4 × 10–20 | 60 s | 40–60 | A clean pop every touch, steady rhythm |
| 2 | Travel | Pogos forward/back and side to side over a line | 4–5 × 15–20 | 60–90 s | 60–80 | Same gate while moving |
| 3 | Load and go | Snap-down straight into a jump, no pause; reach-and-rip jumps | 4–6 × 3–5, plus 2 × 15 pogos | 90 s | 50–70 | No pause at the bottom |
| 4 | One leg | Single-leg pogos; hop and stick; side hop and stick | 3–4 × 8–12 each leg | 90 s | 50–80 | Hips level, trunk over the foot, sides close |
| 5 | Rebound (adults) | Max-intent pogos; continuous rebound jumps, no box | 4–5 × 6–10 | 2 min | 40–60 | Rhythm and pogo score hold set to set |
| 5C | Drop (coach-unlocked adults) | Low drop jump 20–30 cm, "hot floor" | 3–4 × 4–6 | 2 min | 15–25 | Unlocked by me only |
| 6 | Approach | 1-, 3-, 5-step approach jumps; penultimate step; dunk attempts | 4–6 × 3–5 | 2–3 min | 20–30 max-effort | Approach jump beats your standing jump |

**End the set the moment:** the pop turns into a thud, the knees bend more than a little, the heels sink or the foot tripod collapses (big toe, little toe and heel stop sharing the load), the rhythm stutters, or anything hurts. When the app measures contacts, it ends the set when the 3-contact average contact time rises more than 10% above the set's best 3.

**Pop, not thud. Bounce the ball, don't place it.**

### 8.2 The plumb line and the seated rib turn (both are self-checks, never pass or fail)
- **Plumb line:** side-on photo, phone at hip height, 8–10 ft away, level, barefoot, 3 quiet seconds. A vertical line from just in front of your ankle bone: where do your knee, hip, shoulder and ear sit? Trend it every 14 days. There are no published "too far" values, so we compare you to you.
- **Seated rib turn:** knees squeezing a ball, phone flat on your breastbone, turn each way. My coach target is more than 45° each way. A big left-right gap matters more than the number.

**Turn from the ribs, not the low back.** Your upper back is built to turn and your low back is built to stay steady. The idea that the spine and trunk are part of the motor, not just a frame, comes from Serge Gracovetsky's "spinal engine" theory (1988). Later research backs the idea that the trunk matters, not the claim that it's the main engine.

## 9. Track 3 · Dunking & Plyometrics

### 9.1 Arm swing and approach
- **Arm swing score:** jump hands-on-hips, then with a full arm swing. The difference is your score. Re-test every 14 days. Arm swing is free height; we measure your own gap.
- **Two-foot jumpers:** "Reach back on the dip, rip up through the rim."
- **One-foot jumpers:** "Free knee and arm rise together. Let the ribs swing against the hips." "Knee up, hip up."
- **Penultimate step:** "Long, low second-to-last step. Quick last step." Film it with the recorder.
- Common fixes: arms stop at the shoulders, "finish at the rim, not your face"; arms late, "arms back as you drop"; shoulders shrug, "long neck, big swing."

### 9.2 One-foot jumper side-strength ladder
Suitcase carry → offset-load march → side plank with top-hip hike → step-up with free-knee drive and hip hike → single-leg pogos → 3- to 5-step one-foot approach jumps. Step back a rung if your trunk leans past your foot or your pelvis drops.

## 11. Deloads and auto-regulation
- **Planned deload:** week 4.
- **Earned deload:** if your jump height or pogo score is more than 5% below your own 3-session average two sessions in a row, cut plyo contacts by about half and drop the heaviest sets that week. Keep breath, Down, Up, Check and technique.
- **Dunk session stop:** rest or finish when your last 3 attempts average more than 10% under your top 3 for the session. Two flags and you're done for the day.
- **Lifts with bar-speed tracking:** end a set after 2 reps in a row slower than about 10% off your fastest rep on power days, or about 20% on strength days.

## 12. Recovery
- Sleep is the pillar that decides the others. Aim for a consistent bedtime.
- Eat enough protein and carbohydrate to recover from training.
- If you use collagen, take it with vitamin C about an hour before tendon work. It's optional.
- Every session ends with the Neural Flush.
