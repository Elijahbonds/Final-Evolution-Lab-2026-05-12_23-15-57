# Camp Blueprint — Facilitator Curriculum

Owner's document, received 3 Sep 2026. This is the source of truth for the
Camp's curriculum; `lib/camp/curriculum.ts` is its typed mirror and the Camp
view renders from that module. Edit here first, then the module.

**Program type:** 8-week mentorship cycle, in-person + in-app hybrid
**Deliverable:** a populated Shared Profile the mentee keeps and carries
**Designed for replication:** any trained facilitator can run this, not just the author

---

## The Thesis (say this out loud to mentees in week 1)

You cannot test whether your plan for becoming what you want to be is
working. That loop is years long. Training has a loop that closes in
weeks. So we use training to practice the moves — set a goal, measure
honestly, build a plan, execute through the stall, prove the delta —
and then we move those same moves onto the thing you actually want.

We are not claiming squats make you an architect. We are claiming that
the person who knows how to run a plan through a plateau is the person
who becomes an architect, and this is the fastest place to learn that.

---

## Two Tracks, Running in Parallel

### Career Track — long loop, 1 session/week
The declared destination, reverse-engineered into a real pathway map.
Low frequency, high thinking.

### Movement Track — short loop, 3 sessions/week
A movement goal the mentee picks themselves. System Scan → gap →
programmed sessions → re-scan. High frequency, high reps.

### The Bridge — 10 minutes, every single week
The facilitator names the transfer out loud. Without this, the program
is a fitness class sitting next to a career worksheet. This is not
optional and it is not something to improvise at the end if there's
time. Put it on the clock.

## Eight-Week Arc

### Week 1 — DECLARE
**Output:** a written destination with a name attached.

Not "get in shape." Not "be successful." A role a person could hold.
If the mentee doesn't have one, they pick a placeholder and we proceed
anyway — the plan is the lesson, not the accuracy of the guess.

**Facilitator script:**
- "What do you want to be? Not what you're supposed to say. What?"
- "If that doesn't work out, what's the second one?"
- "Who's someone who already does this? Do you know their path?"
- "You can change this in week five. Pick one for now."

**Trap to avoid:** do not correct or downgrade the dream. A 14-year-old
who says "NBA" gets the NBA pathway mapped honestly, including the
numbers. The map does the correcting, not you.

### Week 2 — MEASURE
**Output:** Movement Signature + PRQ baseline, on file.

Run the System Scan and movement screen. This is the day the mentee
gets real data about their own body, most of them for the first time.
Handle it carefully — findings are described as a starting point, never
as a verdict, and never as a medical finding.

**Facilitator script:**
- "This is a baseline, not a grade."
- "The number matters less than the direction it moves."

**Label discipline:** all scan output is "estimated engagement," never
clinical measurement. No diagnosing. No injury opinions. Refer out.

### Week 3 — MAP THE GAP
**Output:** two gap documents — career and movement.

This is the hardest facilitator skill in the program and the step where
a weak mentor hands out something generic. See the Pathway Map Protocol
below — the facilitator is not expected to already know how to become a
marine biologist.

### Week 4 — BUILD THE PLAN
**Output:** a dated timeline for each track, with checkpoints.

Career track gets checkpoints on a multi-year timeline with the first
three inside 90 days. Movement track gets an 8-week program with
weekly progressions, delivered through the app with demos and cueing so
the mentee can run it without the facilitator present.

**The mentee writes the plan. The facilitator edits it.** If the
facilitator writes it, the mentee learns nothing and won't run it.

### Weeks 5–6 — EXECUTE
Reps. The facilitator's job shrinks to enforcement, form, and the
weekly Bridge conversation.

### Week 6 — THE PLATEAU (scheduled, not accidental)
Almost everyone stalls around here. Treat it as curriculum, not as
failure. If a mentee somehow hasn't stalled, manufacture the lesson by
raising the load until they do.

**Facilitator script:**
- "This was going to happen. It happens to everyone. What now?"
- "Quit, wait, or adjust. Those are the three. Which are you picking?"
- "What would you adjust — the load, the frequency, or the goal?"

**This is the single most important session in the program.** The
whole thesis lives or dies here. Do not rush it, do not rescue them
out of it, and do not let them quit inside it.

### Week 7 — ADJUST
**Output:** a revised plan, with the revision reason written down.

Plan revision is a skill, not an admission of failure. The written
reason is the artifact — it's evidence the mentee can reason about
their own process.

### Week 8 — PROVE
**Output:** re-scan, delta against baseline, Creator Card issued,
Shared Profile updated and handed over.

The mentee presents: where they started, what they changed, what moved,
and what their next checkpoint is. Present to the group, or to a parent,
or to camera. The presentation matters — articulating your own process
is the transferable skill.

## The Pathway Map Protocol

A facilitator cannot improvise the path to every career. Don't try.
Run this protocol *with* the mentee — them learning to map a path
themselves is better content than you already knowing it.

For any declared destination, fill in six fields:

1. **Entry points** — how do people actually get in? Name at least
   three different routes, because there is never only one.
2. **Credential required** — degree, license, portfolio, audition,
   certification, or nothing. Be accurate. Many careers require less
   formal schooling than a teenager assumes.
3. **First real step** — something doable inside 90 days. An email,
   a class, a piece of work made, a person contacted.
4. **Who already does this** — a named person, ideally reachable.
   Local beats famous.
5. **The honest numbers** — how many people attempt this, how many
   land it, what it pays at year one and year ten. Present without
   spin in either direction.
6. **The second path** — what the same skills open if the first
   doesn't happen. This is not a consolation prize. It's insurance,
   and framing it that way keeps the dream intact.

The facilitator's job is to make sure all six get filled, not to fill
them personally.

## The Bridge — Weekly Transfer Prompts

Rotate these. The mentee answers, not the facilitator.

- "You stalled this week and you adjusted. Where else in your life do
  you need to make that same move?"
- "You measured before you planned. What are you currently guessing at
  that you could measure instead?"
- "Your program says three sessions. You did two. What actually got in
  the way, and is that same thing in the way of the big goal?"
- "You just did something hard on a day you didn't want to. Name the
  next time you'll need that."
- "What's the difference between the version of you who finishes this
  and the version who doesn't?"

## What We Measure

**Honest and reportable:**
- Movement Signature delta, baseline to week 8
- PRQ delta
- Session adherence rate
- Career-track checkpoints completed
- Plan revisions made after a stall (a *positive* metric)
- Whether the mentee can state their own next step unprompted

**Do not claim:**
- Career outcomes. The loop is longer than the camp. Saying otherwise
  is dishonest and it's the thing that will get the program dismissed
  by the schools and orgs you want to sell into.

---

## Facilitator Standards

- You are a facilitator, not a therapist and not a clinician. Refer out
  for anything medical, anything psychological, anything at home.
- Never downgrade a mentee's stated dream. Map it honestly instead.
- The mentee writes; you edit.
- The Bridge conversation happens every week without exception.
- All scan and screen output is estimated engagement, never diagnosis.
- If you don't know a career path, say so and run the protocol together.
  Modeling "I don't know, here's how I'd find out" is the lesson.

---

## Replication Package

To hand this to another facilitator, they need:
1. This document
2. The Pathway Map Protocol as a fillable worksheet
3. The eight-week session plans with scripts
4. App access for scan, programming, demos, and Profile
5. A trained observation — they run one cycle shadowed before running
   one alone

The lookup table of pre-built pathway maps is a growing asset, not a
prerequisite. Every cycle run adds maps to it. After enough cycles it
becomes the moat.
